\section{Physical Modelling}
\label{sec:physical_modelling}

\subsection{Kinematics}
First task is to derive the direct kinematics of the Core XY mechanism. One fact that slightly complicates this is the fact that rotating one motor will move the tool head in both $x$ and $y$ directions. By removing the right motor, we can see that by moving the tool head in the positive $x$ direction \textbf{or} in the positive $y$ direction, the left motor will rotate in the positive direction. We can draw similar conclusions for the right motor, for which we get the following equations:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:belt_kinematics}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta L &= \Delta x + \Delta y \\
\Delta R &= \Delta x - \Delta y \\
\Delta x &= \frac{\Delta L + \Delta R}{2} \\
\Delta y &= \frac{\Delta L - \Delta R}{2}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

With $\Delta L$ and $\Delta R$ being the length of belt moved by the motors, and $\Delta x$ and $\Delta y$ being the change in position of the tool head.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:belt_motor}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta L &= r_m \, \theta_L \\
\Delta R &= r_m \, \theta_R
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

If we assume that in the beginning the motors are at
$\theta_L = \theta_R = 0$ and the cart is at $(0, 0)$, we get the following equations:



\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kinematics}
\begin{aligned}
x(\theta_L, \theta_R) &= \frac{r_m \theta_L + r_m \theta_R}{2}
  = \frac{r_m}{2} \left( \theta_L + \theta_R \right) \\
y(\theta_L, \theta_R) &= \frac{r_m \theta_L - r_m \theta_R}{2}
  = \frac{r_m}{2} \left( \theta_L - \theta_R \right)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kinematics_vel}
\begin{aligned}
\dot{x}(\dot{\theta}_L, \dot{\theta}_R) &= \frac{r_m}{2} \left( \dot{\theta}_L + \dot{\theta}_R \right) \\
\dot{y}(\dot{\theta}_L, \dot{\theta}_R) &= \frac{r_m}{2} \left( \dot{\theta}_L - \dot{\theta}_R \right)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Dynamics}
In the next part we model the dynamics of the 3D printer system by applying the Euler-Lagrange equation. Additionally, we will split the problem to a mechanical and an electrical one, with the angular velocity of the motor shafts connecting them. 

Because of that, we choose generalized coordinates:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gen_coords}
q = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ q_L \\ q_R \end{bmatrix}
\qquad
\dot q = \begin{bmatrix} \dot x \\ \dot y \\ i_L \\ i_R \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
We can also then split them among our two problems:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gen_coords_split}
\dot q_m = \begin{bmatrix} \dot x \\ \dot y \end{bmatrix} \ \text{(mechanical part)},
\qquad
\dot q_e = \begin{bmatrix} i_L \\ i_R \end{bmatrix} \ \text{(electrical part)}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Mechanical part}
\subsubsection*{Kinetic energy}
Firstly, we derive the kinetic energy terms. In the \textit{mechanical} part, the only mass is the tool head. With that we have:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kinetic}
K_{mech} = \tfrac{1}{2} m (\dot x^2 + \dot y^2)
\end{equation}

As the whole system is planar, we can see that there is no potential energy terms as it will be constant.

\subsubsection*{Dissipative energy}
Secondly, we find the dissipative energy terms. Each motor has 4 rotational bearings, and 4 linear bearings for the whole system, two along each axis. We define $r_b$ as the bearing radius, later it will be kept the same as the motor radius for simplicity. From \eqref{eq:kinematics}, we can get transformations \eqref{eq:inverse_kinematics} with trivial time derivatives.  
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:inverse_kinematics}
\theta_{b,L} = \frac{ x +  y}{r_b},
\qquad
\theta_{b,R} = \frac{ x - y}{r_b}
\end{equation}
Then, for each side we get rotational dissipative energy:
\begin{equation*}
\label{eq:dissipation_sides}
\begin{aligned}
D_L &= 4\cdot \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot \dot\theta_{b,L}^2\,\mu_r 
    = 2\mu_r \frac{1}{r_b^2}\left[\dot x^2 + 2\dot x\dot y + \dot y^2\right] \\
D_R &= 4\cdot \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot \dot\theta_{b,R}^2\,\mu_r 
    = 2\mu_r \frac{1}{r_b^2}\left[\dot x^2 - 2\dot x\dot y + \dot y^2\right]
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

Then simply combining them:
\begin{equation*}
\label{eq:dissipation_rot}
\boxed{D_{\mathrm{rot}} = 4\,\frac{\mu_r}{r_b^2}\left[\dot x^2 + \dot y^2\right]}
\end{equation*}

Similarly, we get the linear dissipative energy:
\begin{equation*}
\label{eq:dissipation_lin}
D_{\mathrm{lin}} = 2\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{l,x}\,\dot x^2 + 2\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{l,y}\,\dot y^2 = \mu_{l,x}\,\dot x^2 + \mu_{l,y}\,\dot y^2
\end{equation*}

Then, combining the terms we get:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dissipation_mech}
D_m = \left[4\,\frac{\mu_r}{r_b^2} + \mu_{l,x}\right]\dot x^2
    + \left[4\,\frac{\mu_r}{r_b^2} + \mu_{l,y}\right]\dot y^2
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Electromechanical part}
Now we model the energies of the electrical part of the system. Keep in mind that both motors are the same. Below we can see the equivalent circuit of a DC motor:

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{motor_sch.png}
  \caption{Equivalent circuit of a DC motor.}
  \label{fig:motor_circuit}
\end{figure}

\subsubsection*{Kinetic energy}
The inductor and the inertial element will contribute to the kinetic energy of this part of the system. Similarly, like \eqref{eq:inverse_kinematics}, using the motor shaft radius:

\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
K_L &= \tfrac{1}{2} i_L^2 L_m + \tfrac{1}{2} J_m \dot\theta_L^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} i_L^2 L_m + \tfrac{1}{2} J_m \frac{1}{r_m^2}\left(\dot x^2 + \dot y^2 + 2\dot x\dot y\right) \\
K_R &= \tfrac{1}{2} i_R^2 L_m + \tfrac{1}{2} J_m \frac{1}{r_m^2}\left(\dot x^2 + \dot y^2 - 2\dot x\dot y\right) \\
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

Combining them we get:

\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:kin_el}
  K_{EL} = \tfrac{1}{2} L_m \left[i_R^2 + i_L^2\right] + J_m \frac{1}{r_m^2}\left(\dot x^2 + \dot y^2\right)
\end{equation}

As there are no capacitors in the system, there is no potential energy change as in the mechanical part.

\subsubsection*{Dissipative energy}
Only dissipative term in the electrical part of the system is the resistor:

\begin{equation}
  \begin{gathered}
    D_{EL,L} = \tfrac{1}{2} i_L^2 R_m, \qquad 
    D_{EL,R} = \tfrac{1}{2} i_R^2 R_m \\
    D_{EL} = \tfrac{1}{2} R_m \left(i_L^2 + i_R^2\right)
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{Matrix equations}
Finally, we realize that the system is linear, and write the matrix expressions:

\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    K = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\dot q^\top \mathbf{M}\,\dot q,
\qquad
D = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\dot q^\top \mathbf{N}\,\dot q
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

With corresponding matrices:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{M} &= \begin{bmatrix}
m + J_m \dfrac{2}{r_m^2} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & m + J_m \dfrac{2}{r_m^2} & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & L_m & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & L_m
\end{bmatrix} \\
\mathbf{N} &= \begin{bmatrix}
8\dfrac{\mu_r}{r_b^2} + 2\mu_{l,x} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 8\dfrac{\mu_r}{r_b^2} + 2\mu_{l,y} & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & R_m & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & R_m
\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}


\subsubsection{Generalized force}
\subsubsection*{Mechanical coordinates}
The non-conservative forces in the mechanical part are given by the motor torques $\tau_L$ and $\tau_R$. The virtual work they perform is:
\begin{equation*}
\Delta W = \tau_L\,\Delta\theta_L + \tau_R\,\Delta\theta_R
\end{equation*}
With the familiar transformation, analogous to \eqref{eq:inverse_kinematics} but using the motor radius $r_m$, we get:
\begin{equation*}
\Delta W = \frac{\tau_L + \tau_R}{r_m}\,\Delta x + \frac{\tau_L - \tau_R}{r_m}\,\Delta y
\end{equation*}
Applying the motor law $\tau = K_m i$, the generalized forces for $x$ and $y$ are:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gen_force_mech}
Q_x = \frac{K_m}{r_m}\left(i_L + i_R\right),
\qquad
Q_y = \frac{K_m}{r_m}\left(i_L - i_R\right)
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{Electrical coordinates}
The non-conservative forces in the electrical part are the input voltages and the back-EMF of the motors:
\begin{equation*}
\Delta W = \left(u_{m,L} - K_m\dot\theta_L\right)\Delta q_L + \left(u_{m,R} - K_m\dot\theta_R\right)\Delta q_R
\end{equation*}
Using the same transformation, we get the generalized forces for $q_L$ and $q_R$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gen_force_el}
Q_{q_L} = u_{m,L} - \frac{K_m}{r_m}\left(\dot x + \dot y\right),
\qquad
Q_{q_R} = u_{m,R} - \frac{K_m}{r_m}\left(\dot x - \dot y\right)
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{Lagrange equations}
Using the fact this is a linear system and there are no potential energies we get:


\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{L} = K - V = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\dot q^\top \mathbf{M}\,\dot q - V,
\qquad V = 0
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\mathcal{L} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\dot q^\top \mathbf{M}\,\dot q
\end{equation*}

\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q} &= \frac{d}{dt}\left(\mathbf{M}\dot q\right) = \mathbf{M}\ddot q \\
\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q} &= 0 \\
\frac{\partial D}{\partial \dot q} &= \mathbf{N}\dot q
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

And finally solving the Euler-Lagrange equation:

\begin{equation*}
\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q} - \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q} = -\frac{\partial D}{\partial \dot q} + \mathbf{Q},
\qquad
\mathbf{Q} = \begin{bmatrix} Q_x \\ Q_y \\ Q_{q_L} \\ Q_{q_R} \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation*}

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lagrange_matrix}
\mathbf{M}\ddot q + \mathbf{N}\dot q = \mathbf{Q}
\end{equation}

Expanding back out of matrix form we get:


\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\left(m + J_m\frac{2}{r_m^2}\right)\ddot x
  + \left(8\frac{\mu_r}{r_b^2} + 2\mu_{l,x}\right)\dot x
  - \frac{K_m}{r_m} i_L - \frac{K_m}{r_m} i_R &= 0 \\
\left(m + J_m\frac{2}{r_m^2}\right)\ddot y
  + \left(8\frac{\mu_r}{r_b^2} + 2\mu_{l,y}\right)\dot y
  - \frac{K_m}{r_m} i_L + \frac{K_m}{r_m} i_R &= 0 \\
L_m \frac{di_L}{dt} + R_m i_L + \frac{K_m}{r_m}\dot x + \frac{K_m}{r_m}\dot y - u_{m,L} &= 0 \\
L_m \frac{di_R}{dt} + R_m i_R + \frac{K_m}{r_m}\dot x - \frac{K_m}{r_m}\dot y - u_{m,R} &= 0
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

Or with substitutions, we get final form of the four differential equations:

\begin{equation*}
m_{eq} = m + \frac{2J_m}{r_m^2},
\qquad
b_x = 8\frac{\mu_r}{r_b^2} + 2\mu_{l,x},
\qquad
b_y = 8\frac{\mu_r}{r_b^2} + 2\mu_{l,y}
\end{equation*}

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:state_equations}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\ddot x &= -\frac{b_x}{m_{eq}}\dot x + \frac{K_m}{m_{eq} r_m}\left(i_L + i_R\right) \\
\ddot y &= -\frac{b_y}{m_{eq}}\dot y + \frac{K_m}{m_{eq} r_m}\left(i_L - i_R\right) \\
\frac{di_L}{dt} &= -\frac{K_m}{r_m L_m}\left(\dot x + \dot y\right) - \frac{R_m}{L_m} i_L + \frac{u_{m,L}}{L_m} \\
\frac{di_R}{dt} &= -\frac{K_m}{r_m L_m}\left(\dot x - \dot y\right) - \frac{R_m}{L_m} i_R + \frac{u_{m,R}}{L_m}
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}

Or in \textbf{state-space} form:

\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ \dot x \\ \dot y \\ i_L \\ i_R \end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\dot{\mathbf{x}} = \begin{bmatrix} \dot x \\ \dot y \\ \ddot x \\ \ddot y \\ \dfrac{di_L}{dt} \\[1ex] \dfrac{di_R}{dt} \end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mathbf{u} = \begin{bmatrix} u_{m,L} \\ u_{m,R} \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation*}

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:state_space}
\dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{u}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{A} =
\begin{bmatrix}
\mstrut 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
\mstrut 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
\mstrut 0 & 0 & -\dfrac{b_x}{m_{eq}} & 0 & \dfrac{K_m}{m_{eq} r_m} & \dfrac{K_m}{m_{eq} r_m} \\
\mstrut 0 & 0 & 0 & -\dfrac{b_y}{m_{eq}} & \dfrac{K_m}{m_{eq} r_m} & -\dfrac{K_m}{m_{eq} r_m} \\
\mstrut 0 & 0 & -\dfrac{K_m}{r_m L_m} & -\dfrac{K_m}{r_m L_m} & -\dfrac{R_m}{L_m} & 0 \\
\mstrut 0 & 0 & -\dfrac{K_m}{r_m L_m} & \dfrac{K_m}{r_m L_m} & 0 & -\dfrac{R_m}{L_m}
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mathbf{B} =
\begin{bmatrix}
\mstrut 0 & 0 \\ \mstrut 0 & 0 \\ \mstrut 0 & 0 \\ \mstrut 0 & 0 \\ \mstrut \dfrac{1}{L_m} & 0 \\ \mstrut 0 & \dfrac{1}{L_m}
\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation*}

\subsubsection{Decoupling the x and y dynamics}
To decouple the system, we introduce a set of useful substitutions:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decoupling_subs}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
i_x &= i_L + i_R, & u_{m,x} &= u_{m,L} + u_{m,R} \\
i_y &= i_L - i_R, & u_{m,y} &= u_{m,L} - u_{m,R} \\
i_L &= \frac{i_x + i_y}{2}, & u_{m,L} &= \frac{u_{m,x} + u_{m,y}}{2} \\
i_R &= \frac{i_x - i_y}{2}, & u_{m,R} &= \frac{u_{m,x} - u_{m,y}}{2}
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}

By substituting our new decoupled currents we get:

\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{di_L}{dt} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\frac{di_x}{dt} + \tfrac{1}{2}\frac{di_y}{dt}
  = -\frac{K_m}{r_m L_m}\dot x - \frac{K_m}{r_m L_m}\dot y
    - \frac{R_m}{2L_m} i_x - \frac{R_m}{2L_m} i_y + \frac{1}{2L_m}\left(u_{m,x} + u_{m,y}\right) \\
\frac{di_R}{dt} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\frac{di_x}{dt} - \tfrac{1}{2}\frac{di_y}{dt}
  = -\frac{K_m}{r_m L_m}\dot x + \frac{K_m}{r_m L_m}\dot y
    - \frac{R_m}{2L_m} i_x + \frac{R_m}{2L_m} i_y + \frac{1}{2L_m}\left(u_{m,x} - u_{m,y}\right)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

Then by adding and subtracting the two equations to cancel terms we get:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decoupled_currents}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{di_x}{dt} &= -\frac{2K_m}{r_m L_m}\dot x - \frac{R_m}{L_m} i_x + \frac{1}{L_m} u_{m,x} \\
\frac{di_y}{dt} &= -\frac{2K_m}{r_m L_m}\dot y - \frac{R_m}{L_m} i_y + \frac{1}{L_m} u_{m,y}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

And by substituting \eqref{eq:decoupling_subs} into the first two equations of \eqref{eq:state_equations}:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decoupled_motion}
\begin{aligned}
\ddot x &= -\frac{b_x}{m_{eq}}\dot x + \frac{K_m}{m_{eq} r_m} i_x \\
\ddot y &= -\frac{b_y}{m_{eq}}\dot y + \frac{K_m}{m_{eq} r_m} i_y
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

And finally:

\begin{equation*}
\tilde{\mathbf{x}} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ \dot x \\ i_x \\ y \\ \dot y \\ i_y \end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\dot{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}} = \begin{bmatrix} \dot x \\ \ddot x \\ \dfrac{di_x}{dt} \\[1ex] \dot y \\ \ddot y \\ \dfrac{di_y}{dt} \end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\tilde{\mathbf{u}} = \begin{bmatrix} u_{m,x} \\ u_{m,y} \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation*}

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:state_space_decoupled}
\dot{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}} = \tilde{\mathbf{A}}\,\tilde{\mathbf{x}} + \tilde{\mathbf{B}}\,\tilde{\mathbf{u}}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation*}
\tilde{\mathbf{A}} =
\left[\begin{array}{ccc|ccc}
\mstrut 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
\mstrut 0 & -\dfrac{b_x}{m_{eq}} & \dfrac{K_m}{m_{eq} r_m} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
\mstrut 0 & -\dfrac{2K_m}{r_m L_m} & -\dfrac{R_m}{L_m} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ \hline
\mstrut 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\
\mstrut 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & -\dfrac{b_y}{m_{eq}} & \dfrac{K_m}{m_{eq} r_m} \\
\mstrut 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & -\dfrac{2K_m}{r_m L_m} & -\dfrac{R_m}{L_m}
\end{array}\right],
\qquad
\tilde{\mathbf{B}} =
\begin{bmatrix}
\mstrut 0 & 0 \\ \mstrut 0 & 0 \\ \mstrut \dfrac{1}{L_m} & 0 \\ \mstrut 0 & 0 \\ \mstrut 0 & 0 \\ \mstrut 0 & \dfrac{1}{L_m}
\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Drawing shapes}
\label{sec:drawing_shapes}

Rearranging the decoupled equations of motion \eqref{eq:decoupled_motion}, we can express the currents needed to follow a prescribed motion of the tool head:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:inverse_currents}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
i_x &= \frac{m_{eq} r_m}{K_m}\,\ddot x + \frac{b_x r_m}{K_m}\,\dot x \\
i_y &= \frac{m_{eq} r_m}{K_m}\,\ddot y + \frac{b_y r_m}{K_m}\,\dot y
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}

The motor equations for each motor are:

\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
u_{m,L} &= L_m \frac{di_L}{dt} + R_m i_L + K_m \dot\theta_L
         = L_m \frac{di_L}{dt} + R_m i_L + \frac{K_m}{r_m}\left(\dot x + \dot y\right) \\
u_{m,R} &= L_m \frac{di_R}{dt} + R_m i_R + \frac{K_m}{r_m}\left(\dot x - \dot y\right)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

Adding them, and using \eqref{eq:decoupling_subs}:

\begin{equation*}
u_{m,x} = u_{m,L} + u_{m,R}
        = L_m\left[\frac{di_L}{dt} + \frac{di_R}{dt}\right] + R_m\left[i_L + i_R\right] + \frac{2K_m}{r_m}\,\dot x
\end{equation*}

and similarly by subtracting them, we get the decoupled input voltages, consistent with \eqref{eq:decoupled_currents}:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:inverse_voltages}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
u_{m,x} &= L_m \frac{di_x}{dt} + R_m i_x + \frac{2K_m}{r_m}\,\dot x \\
u_{m,y} &= L_m \frac{di_y}{dt} + R_m i_y + \frac{2K_m}{r_m}\,\dot y
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}

The original motor voltages then follow from \eqref{eq:decoupling_subs}:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:inverse_motor_voltages}
u_{m,L} = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(u_{m,x} + u_{m,y}\right),
\qquad
u_{m,R} = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(u_{m,x} - u_{m,y}\right)
\end{equation}

Since \eqref{eq:inverse_voltages} contains $\frac{di_x}{dt}$, and \eqref{eq:inverse_currents} contains $\ddot x$, the voltages depend on the third derivative of the position. Therefore, for any $C^3$ functions $x(t)$ and $y(t)$ describing the position of the tool head, we can derive an open-loop analytical solution for the input voltages, even if those voltages aren't realistic.

\subsubsection*{Ellipse}
An ellipse with centre $(h, k)$ and semi-axes $a$ and $b$ has the parametric form:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ellipse}
x(t) = h + a\cos t,
\qquad
y(t) = k + b\sin t
\end{equation}

We also need the derivatives up to the third order:

\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\dot x &= -a\sin t, & \ddot x &= -a\cos t, & \dddot x &= a\sin t \\
\dot y &= b\cos t,  & \ddot y &= -b\sin t, & \dddot y &= -b\cos t
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

Substituting into \eqref{eq:inverse_currents}:

\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
i_x(t) &= -\frac{m_{eq} r_m}{K_m}\,a\cos t - \frac{b_x r_m}{K_m}\,a\sin t
        = -\frac{r_m}{K_m}\,a\left[m_{eq}\cos t + b_x\sin t\right] \\
i_y(t) &= \frac{r_m}{K_m}\,b\left[b_y\cos t - m_{eq}\sin t\right]
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

And into \eqref{eq:inverse_voltages}:

\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
u_{m,x}(t) &= L_m\frac{r_m}{K_m}\,a\left[m_{eq}\sin t - b_x\cos t\right]
            - R_m\frac{r_m}{K_m}\,a\left[m_{eq}\cos t + b_x\sin t\right]
            - \frac{2K_m}{r_m}\,a\sin t \\
u_{m,y}(t) &= -L_m\frac{r_m}{K_m}\,b\left[m_{eq}\cos t + b_y\sin t\right]
            + R_m\frac{r_m}{K_m}\,b\left[b_y\cos t - m_{eq}\sin t\right]
            + \frac{2K_m}{r_m}\,b\cos t
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

With
\begin{equation*}
u_{m,L}(t) = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(u_{m,x}(t) + u_{m,y}(t)\right),
\qquad
u_{m,R}(t) = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(u_{m,x}(t) - u_{m,y}(t)\right)
\end{equation*}
we obtain the analytical open-loop solution for drawing the ellipse.
